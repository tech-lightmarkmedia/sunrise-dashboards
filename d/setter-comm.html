Dashboard Guide

What this dashboard shows

Communication activity for Sunrise's four setters — Kayla Daughtrey, Cierra Milao, Alex White, and Aidan Conner — calls, SMS, and email. Calls are broken out by HubSpot call disposition (Connected, Left voicemail, Left live message, No answer, Busy, Wrong number, Connect - Scheduled, Connected - Not Engaged).

Why dispositions are shown separately, not rolled into a "connect rate"

Different setters may disposition calls differently in practice — one might lean on "Left live message" while another leans on "Left voicemail," and not everyone uses every disposition equally. Rather than collapse dispositions into a single connect/no-connect metric (which would bake in an assumption about which dispositions "count"), each disposition is reported on its own with its own count, share of total, average duration, and median duration. Read them side by side rather than looking for one headline number.

Average vs. median duration

Both are shown per disposition because average alone can mislead — a handful of long calls pull the average up, and dispositions like "No answer" are mechanically near-zero seconds. Median is less sensitive to outliers and is usually the more representative number for a typical call under that disposition.

Data source and freshness

Calls are read from the worker's /cached-calls R2 cache, which covers the trailing 180 days and rebuilds nightly at 2 AM Eastern. This dashboard does not fetch live from HubSpot, so a call logged in the last few hours may not appear until the next rebuild. There is no way to look back further than 180 days on this dashboard.

Date ranges and comparison

  • Yesterday, This/Last Week, This/Last Quarter, Last 180 Days — fixed presets.
  • Custom — pick any from/to date.
  • Quarters are calendar quarters (Jan–Mar, Apr–Jun, Jul–Sep, Oct–Dec).
  • All date boundaries are pinned to America/New_York, so the range means the same thing regardless of the viewer's own timezone.

The vs picker compares the selected range against either the immediately preceding period of equal length, or the same calendar period one year prior. If the current range is a still-in-progress period (This Week, This Quarter), the comparison period is truncated to the same elapsed span for a fair comparison.

Combined vs. Side by Side

Each section (Outbound / Inbound) can show a single combined table for all four setters together, or tables side by side — one per setter. The Setter filter in the top bar narrows everything (including the combined view) to a single person if selected.

Average Daily Phone Activity

One card per setter, combining outbound and inbound calls into a single figure: total talk time per active day, shown as a percentage of an 8-hour workday. This is deliberately per-person, not blended across all four setters — a combined average could mask uneven activity between people. "Active day" here means any day with at least one logged call, inbound or outbound.

Read this alongside the caveat below it. This only counts phone activity that HubSpot logged a disposition for. It says nothing about email, SMS, internal meetings, or other parts of the setter role — a low percentage here means low logged phone time specifically, not necessarily low overall output. Treat it as a starting point for a conversation, not a verdict.

Telephone Activity chart

A stacked chart of total call volume by day across the selected date range, broken out by disposition. Outbound and inbound calls are merged together in this chart — each series is a disposition (Connected, Left voicemail, etc.) regardless of direction — so the top of the stack on any given day is total calls of any kind. If you need the outbound/inbound split, use the sections below instead.

The chart type switches automatically based on the selected range: bars for a range of 7 days or less, an area chart for anything longer. This chart does not show a comparison period, even if one is selected in the "vs" picker above — it reflects the current range only.

Count / Time Spent toggle. Switches the chart's y-axis between raw call count (default) and total call duration in minutes, still stacked by disposition. Stack order (which disposition sits at the bottom) is always based on call count, so switching the metric doesn't reshuffle the stack.

Total time column

The Outbound/Inbound disposition tables include a Total time column — the sum of call duration for that disposition in the current range. This is the same underlying number the chart's "Time Spent" view charts by day; the table shows the range total per disposition instead.

Previous-period comparison on every column

When a comparison period is selected in the "vs" picker, every column in the Outbound/Inbound tables — Count, % of total, Avg duration, Median duration, Total time — shows the prior period's value on a second line beneath the current value, with a percentage-change arrow. "% of total" compares the disposition's share between the two periods (e.g. Connected going from 30% to 42% of calls), not a count.

Per active day line

Below each table, a line reports per-disposition figures divided by the number of active days — days that had at least one call, not the total number of calendar days in the range. This matters for ranges with gaps (weekends, days someone didn't dial): dividing by every calendar day would understate typical daily activity, so the figure reflects a typical day someone was actually working the phones. The line states the denominator explicitly ("÷ 17 active days, out of 21 days in the range") so it's clear which day-count every number on that line was divided by. In Side by Side view, each setter's active-day count is their own, not shared.

SMS Activity

Volume of text messages sent and received through HubSpot (logged via Aloware), by day, for each setter. Unlike calls, SMS has no disposition and no duration — there's nothing to disposition a text with, and "talk time" doesn't apply — so this section only tracks count, split by direction.

Automated messages are excluded, via two layers. First, a real HubSpot field: every SMS has a Creator Type of "HubSpot Workflow" or "Manual." The worker extracts this server-side from hs_communication_body (the HTML field — its plain-text counterpart, hs_body_preview, runs everything together with no separator between the Creator Type value and the message text, which caused a real bug in an earlier version of this filter) into a clean hs_creator_type field, and drops the raw HTML before caching — the full HTML for ~55K records made the SMS cache large enough to hit Cloudflare's resource limit on its own, confirmed live 2026-08-11. "HubSpot Workflow" is excluded outright.

Second — and this is the part that isn't a clean field lookup — "Manual" does not mean a human typed it in the moment. Confirmed live 2026-08-11 that Aloware's own automated drip/cadence sequences also get logged as "Manual," since HubSpot only distinguishes its own native Workflow feature from everything else. So "Manual" messages still go through frequency-based template detection: the message text (with the phone/line/creator-type metadata stripped out, and a personalized greeting like "Hi Michael," normalized so the same template with a different name still matches) repeated 5 or more times across all setters combined is treated as a template and excluded. Frequency is counted cross-setter, not per person, because shared templates were confirmed between Kayla/Cierra and between Alex/Aidan — counted individually, a shared template split evenly between two people might not hit the threshold.

This is a text-matching heuristic layered on top of a real field, not a guaranteed classification — it can't catch a template sent fewer than 5 times total, and it depends on the worker's hs_creator_type/hs_extracted_message_text fields being present in the cache (added 2026-08-11; older cached records without them pass through both filters untouched rather than being incorrectly excluded).

Direction is inferred, not a real HubSpot field. A live property check on the communications object turned up nothing resembling a direction property. Instead, direction is guessed from the logged message text: a message reading "{name} has sent an SMS to {number}" is treated as Outbound (the setter sent it); a message starting with a phone number and "has sent an SMS" (no "to") is treated as Inbound. Anything that matches neither pattern is bucketed as "Unknown" rather than guessed into one side or the other. If HubSpot or Aloware ever changes how these messages get logged, this classification can silently break — if "Unknown" ever becomes a large share of the total, that's the signal to check the raw message text again before trusting the Outbound/Inbound split.

The chart and table both use the same date range, comparison period, and Setter filter as the rest of the dashboard. Clicking a direction's count opens a drilldown listing the individual messages: timestamp, the associated HubSpot contact (resolved live via the native communications→contact association, not cached with the rest of the data — confirmed live 2026-08-10 that this association exists and requires no custom label), a message preview, and a link to the record in HubSpot. If a message has no resolved contact, the drilldown says so rather than guessing.

Below the table, a per active day line reports the same denominator convention used on the call tables — divided by days that had at least one SMS, not every calendar day in the range — with the divisor stated explicitly.

Email Activity

Volume of one-on-one emails between each setter and their contacts, by day. Unlike SMS, email direction is a real HubSpot field (not inferred) — outbound emails the setter sent, and inbound replies from the contact, are distinguished natively. The associated contact is also a direct field on the record, so contact resolution here doesn't depend on a separate association lookup the way calls/SMS do.

Same chart/table shape as SMS — Combined/Side by Side toggle, per-active-day line, drilldown on click showing timestamp, resolved contact, subject line, and a link to the record in HubSpot.

Drilldown

Click a disposition's count to open a drawer listing the individual call records for that disposition in the current range, with a link to the record in HubSpot. Each row also shows the resolved contact (via the native calls→contact association, same mechanism as the SMS drilldown), linked to their HubSpot record. If a call has no resolved contact, the row says so rather than guessing.

Outbound Calls has no drilldown. Setters can make well over a hundred outbound calls a day, so a per-record drawer for that section wouldn't be useful and isn't built. Inbound Calls, SMS, and Email all have drilldown as normal.
